# Performance timing, complete speech, and independent starts

Date: 2026-10-30. This policy applies to all templates and untemplated new creation within the existing production workflow.

Single-completion actions normally take 3–8 seconds, with a 10-second limit. Preparation, completion, and withdrawal belong in one segment. Opening one wrapper or handing over an item does not become an extended performance merely because it makes a pleasant sound. Actual sustained trigger performances normally use 6–12 seconds, up to 15 when content supports it. Reactions/waits normally take 1–4 seconds, at most 5; transitions at most 10. Purpose labels do not override the meaning of an action.

Speech is organized around complete thoughts, normally about 20–35 Chinese characters and 6–12 estimated seconds, potentially containing several natural sentences. Brief responses and their explanations generally stay together. New speech estimated above 15 seconds requires correction through the existing bounded repair. Do not mechanically merge confirmed user text or alter adopted audio.

Total runtime and speech ratios guide creation. Develop meaningful conversation or varied sound content when needed; do not assign every remaining second to slow motion, repeated handoffs, or waiting. Explicit requests for little or no speech remain authoritative. Refresh current template examples while retaining saved-draft compatibility.

New non-speaking designs up to 15 seconds run as one video job instead of automatic five-second subdivisions. The content limit for a simple action remains 10 seconds; 15 is a job ceiling, not a default. Longer saved logical performances can be redesigned into blocks up to 15 seconds. Existing saved five-second parts remain readable and are not automatically merged; regenerate their designs and re-export to change them.

Measure adopted and final processed WAV duration, including tails. Reject speech exceeding 15 seconds with a useful split request; never truncate it. Both runner generation modes enforce the 15-second request ceiling and remove the old silent five-second clamp. Existing frame alignment can turn a 15-second request into 362 frames, approximately 15.083 seconds at 24 fps.

All templates permit at most two consecutive tail references before an independent start: `new → continuous → continuous → new`. Earlier explicit independent shots reset the cadence. An independent start regenerates from the character reference and can keep the established setting and framing. Apply the limit to expanded export jobs, including parts. Explicit continuity requests remain subject to the batch limit, which the editor explains. Runner upgrades must preserve the on-disk manifest and include effective modes in runtime state and cache identity; stale results depending on an older chain must not be reused.

The independent ASMR branch remains H3 text-to-video followed by audio extraction. It receives no character image, main video, or preceding tail frame. It produces the requested foley or room tone, not necessarily white noise. Existing post-processing, speech overlay, non-speaking replacement, and original/enhanced merges remain unchanged.

Verify structure, real DP text semantics, deployment/export, and actual H3 audiovisual quality separately. Passing the earlier stages does not prove final video quality.
